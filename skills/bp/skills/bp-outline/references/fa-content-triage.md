# FA Content Triage

A senior financial advisor (FA) improves a BP by deciding where each fact lives, not
by adding pages. The main deck has one job: win the next meeting. Everything else has
another home. Run this triage before the page map is fixed, and again when reviewing
a finished deck.

## Five destinations

| Destination | Holds | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Main deck | What an investor needs to decide on a next meeting | Does the argument break if this is removed? |
| Appendix | Detail that backs a main-deck claim | Is it the answer to a likely follow-up question? |
| Data room | Records to verify: licence, contracts, bank statements, rosters, the cap-table sheet | Is it a document to check rather than an argument to read? |
| Term sheet | Instrument, rights, mechanics | Does it matter only once the investor has decided to negotiate? |
| Spoken answer | Valuation rationale, sensitive comparisons, anything that needs nuance | Would printing it anchor the conversation against the founder? |

Content that fits none of the five is cut: decoration, vanity counts, weakly sourced
statistics, and derived figures with no plan behind them.

## What the main deck must carry

- The one-line definition, the problem, and proof of the product that earns the
  revenue today — not only the product the founder finds most interesting.
- Traction, how money is made with unit economics, market, competition, how the
  business scales, team, and forecast.
- The ask: amount; use of funds; the measurable milestones the money buys; what else
  the founder wants from investors; one contact action. When the price is already
  fixed (crowdfunding, a set-price round), also the stake and the valuation, stated
  as pre- or post-money.

Present the business as one engine with extensions. A list of many revenue lines at
small scale reads as lack of focus.

## Typical items to move

| Item | Destination | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Registration data: credit code, address, business scope | Appendix (name, date, capital) and data room | Identity is verified in diligence, not argued on a page |
| Round-by-round cap table, per-round or per-investor percentages | Appendix summary and data room | The ask needs only this round's stake and the founder's stake after it |
| Valuation multiples offered as self-justification | Spoken answer | The reader anchors on the least flattering multiple |
| “Same price as the last round”, or any flat- or down-round framing, as a headline | Appendix fact | A pricing fact is not an investment highlight |
| Instrument and legal mechanics (SAFE, convertible note, SPV structure) | Term sheet | They easily contradict the stake printed on the ask page |
| Figures derived by arithmetic alone (monthly burn = raise ÷ 12) | Cut | Every printed number must survive Q&A |
| Full financial tables and the rules used to classify bank records | Appendix | The main deck shows one chart and its reading |
| Partner or logo counts and audience totals not tied to revenue | Cut, or one line | Brochure content |
| Second-hand or off-market statistics | Evidence ledger | A weak source weakens the facts beside it |
| Internal review labels (“founder's statement”, “second-hand”) | Evidence ledger | Page footers say “company data, as of DATE” or “management forecast” |

## What triage must not do

Triage relocates. It never deletes a material fact or improves a number.

- Unfavourable facts an investor would rely on stay in the deck: zero paying users,
  dependence on a single founder, a per-unit result that falls in new markets, an
  unverified core assumption. State each once, plainly, on the page it bears on; the
  detail may sit in the appendix.
- Every number keeps its basis and as-of date. Numbers on different bases are not
  combined into one growth multiple.
- A fact that the founder and the written record state differently is not printed as
  a headline. Keep it in the appendix with its basis noted, and list it for
  confirmation.

## Presentation form

- The headline states the conclusion, with a number when one exists; the section
  label stays small.
- One dominant visual per page. A table larger than about four rows by three columns
  becomes a chart plus its reading, and the table moves to the appendix.
- Plan and scenario are drawn differently (solid versus outlined), and the scenario
  is labelled “scenario, not forecast”.
- The ask page reads money → use → milestone. Each milestone is measurable and
  answers one risk the deck admits is unproven.
- Appendix pages are numbered A1, A2 …, and the main deck never depends on them.
- Source notes appear once per page: source and as-of date, no methodology paragraph.
- The cover carries the name, the one-line definition, the document type, and the
  date. Contact details and QR codes go on the closing page.

## Founder inputs an FA will ask for

Record each missing input as a gap. Never fill one in.

- allocation of proceeds across the uses of funds;
- the three-year target behind SOM;
- names, roles, and backgrounds of partners or key hires;
- repeat purchase, referral, and customer-acquisition cost;
- customers and testimonials that may be named;
- how investors are expected to earn a return (dividend, buyback, next round);
- the legal form of the stake (new shares or a transfer) and how earlier stakes are held.

## Output

Add a triage table to the review report: every item moved, cut, or added, with its
destination and reason. List separately the founder inputs still missing.
