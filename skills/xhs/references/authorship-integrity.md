# Authorship Integrity Contract

`lov-xhs` authors a report for a reader. The report must be original synthesis,
not a disguised copy of a note and not an unsupported expansion of the search
result. This contract applies to report mode only; search-only JSON is source
data and must remain verbatim.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Trace source-derived claims.** Any factual claim about a note's title,
   author, date, interaction count, route, price, permit, or quoted observation
   must come from the current `items` or `notes` envelope and should cite the
   note URL in the report.
2. **Separate source from synthesis.** Mark recommendations, calculations,
   and professional judgment as authored reasoning. Do not present them as a
   statement made by a note author or by Xiaohongshu.
3. **Do not invent missing details.** If the corpus is silent, partial, or
   contradictory, say so. Never fill gaps with plausible-sounding names,
   dates, costs, distances, addresses, phone numbers, or safety claims.
4. **Preserve quotations.** If quoting a note, keep the quoted wording intact
   and identify the author. Paraphrase only when the meaning is preserved.
5. **Date and context matter.** Publication labels and interaction counts are
   snapshot metadata. Treat them as evidence with limits, not permanent truth.
6. **Do not overclaim authority.** A public note is not an official permit,
   medical instruction, legal opinion, road closure notice, or ticket policy.
   For safety-critical decisions, tell the reader what must be verified and
   where the verification boundary lies.
7. **Respect platform and copyright boundaries.** Return source URLs and
   citation context; do not claim redistribution rights or reproduce an entire
   note unnecessarily.
8. **Keep the audience in view.** Use the operator's requested language and
   reading context. Do not let internal search logs, cookie state, or tool
   mechanics dominate the report.

## Required authoring flow

1. Read the evidence corpus and count what was actually collected.
2. Extract claims and group them by agreement, conflict, and silence.
3. Draft the conclusion and executable plan or answer.
4. Add evidence links and clearly mark authored recommendations.
5. Run a final verification pass: every source-derived number, name, route,
   and URL must resolve to the corpus; every unresolved gap must be labeled.

## Failure behavior

If the corpus cannot support the requested answer, deliver a partial report
with the missing evidence and the next collection or verification step. A
small, honest report is better than a complete-looking report built from
unsupported assumptions.
